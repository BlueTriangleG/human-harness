# Other eyes
Summary: Before a view, a draft or a plan goes out, have a few people who do not know the background each look for faults from one angle.
When to open: When about to finalize a draft, give someone an important judgement or plan, or accept what a helper hands in.

## Practices
- Pick two or three angles: can a layperson follow it, what would an expert fault, how would an opponent argue against it.
- For each angle find one person (or send one helper) to look, giving them only the thing itself, not your thinking or the background.
- Sort the faults found into: must change, and may stay (write down why it stays).
- Look once more after changing: do not change the words and leave the problem.

## Common pitfalls
- The people asked to find faults know what you want to hear and say only good things.
- Asking only "is it good" and not "where is it wrong".
