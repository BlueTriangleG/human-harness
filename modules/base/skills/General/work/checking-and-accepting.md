# Checking and accepting
Summary: Before saying "passed", "looked at" or "checked", really look and really compare the evidence, then and there.
When to open: When about to say something passed or is done; to write down a time, a figure or a result; to pass on what a helper or a search concluded; to hand something to someone for acceptance.

## Practices
- Before saying "passed": run it yourself, open it and look, and look at this time's result, not the one you remember.
- Times, figures, results: copy them from the original record (a log, a file, a command's output), not from memory.
- Passing on what a helper or a search concluded: check at least one place against the source; if it does not match, say "not checked".
- Handing over for acceptance: say how you checked, so that someone can check again the same way.

## Common pitfalls
- The helper says "done", and you tell others "done" on its word.
- Looking at last time's result and taking it for this time's.
- Saying "it works" for "it should work".
