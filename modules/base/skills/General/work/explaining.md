# Explaining: so that someone who has not seen the material understands
Summary: To explain something to someone (an explanation, a tutorial, a report), first be clear who is listening and what they want, then give the causes and how each step leads to the next.
When to open: Before explaining something to someone or writing the outline and the draft of an explanation; before handing a draft in; after being told "I did not understand" or "too abstract".

## Practices
1. First write one sentence: who it is for, and what they should know or be able to do after hearing it.
2. Start from what they already know, and explain a new word the first time it appears.
3. For each step say why it is needed, so that one step leads to the next.
4. Give a concrete example with every abstract statement.
5. When done, be for once the person who has not seen the material, and read it from the top.

## Common pitfalls
- Leaving out the background that you know and they do not.
- Opening with the conclusion and never saying why.
- Jargon throughout and not one example.
